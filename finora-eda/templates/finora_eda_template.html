07Growth decomposition · mix vs within

How much of the change in new-customer ticket comes from industry mix, and how much happens inside industries?

If Finora simply acquired more customers from low-paying industries, the average ticket would fall with no change inside any industry (mix). If customers of the same industry started entering lower, it would fall even with a constant mix (within).

7.0 · METHODiMethod

Shapley (midpoint) decomposition of a weighted average — exact, no residual

Average entry ticket A = Σᵢ sᵢ · aᵢ, where sᵢ is industry i's share of new customers and aᵢ its average first-month MRR. Between periods 0 and 1:

ΔA = Σᵢ (sᵢ¹ − sᵢ⁰) · (aᵢ⁰ + aᵢ¹)/2 mix  +  Σᵢ (aᵢ¹ − aᵢ⁰) · (sᵢ⁰ + sᵢ¹)/2 within

Using the average of both periods as weights removes the interaction term, so the two effects add up exactly to the change. A Laspeyres version (with interaction) is reported as a cross-check.

  • Periods: 2022 (Mar–Dec, n = {{dec_n0}}), 2023, 2024 (Jan–Oct, n = {{dec_n1}}).
  • Uncertainty: 2,000 bootstrap resamples of new customers within each period → 90% intervals.
  • Robustness: M0 winsorised at P99 ({{winsor_cap}}); early run-rate (median of positive M0–M2).

What the data shows

  • 2022 → 2024: average entry ticket {{dec_a0}} → {{dec_a1}} ({{dec_delta_pct}}). Within industries {{dec_within}} ({{dec_within_share}}); mix {{dec_mix}} ({{dec_mix_share}}). 90% interval for the within share: {{dec_within_ci}}.
  • Robust to outliers and first-month spikes: within share {{dec_within_share_min}}–{{dec_within_share_max}} across the three variants.
  • Holding 2022's industry mix, 2024's average ticket would have been {{dec_cf}} instead of {{dec_a1}}.
  • 2023 → 2024: change of {{dec34_delta}}; within effect 90% interval {{dec34_ci}} — no measurable change after the 2023 step.

What “within” can and cannot mean

  • Within-industry ≠ price. It bundles everything that moves the ticket inside an industry: plan mix, customer size, discounts, promotions, billing timing.
  • Industry is the only segmentation available; a finer segmentation could reveal mix effects hidden inside industries.

What the data shows

What we suspect

Reasonable patterns that need validation with Finora.

  • amount is billed or collected cash per month, not contracted MRR — so churn, reactivation, expansion and contraction include payment timing.
  • Part of the Feb-22 “new” customers (and possibly a few later ones) are pre-existing customers returning after a gap.
  • The {{step_month}} step-down in entry ticket is consistent with a pricing or packaging change, discounting, or smaller customers inside every industry.
  • The rise in new customers while spend fell is consistent with channels outside the S&M file (organic, referrals, partners), effects beyond a 3-month lag, or a lower entry price.
  • The S&M file was built top-down (whole-percent shares of a total); Freelance may have been reclassified into Team in {{team_break}}; PayrollExpenses behaves like a balancing item.
  • The growth of small ± adjustments and off-grid amounts since 2023 is consistent with indexation, discounts, prorations or usage components.
  • 2022 first-month spikes may be setup fees or prepayments.

What we cannot know

Not in these files — never filled by assumption.

  • Pricing, plans, list vs paid price; contract or subscription value; billing frequency.

  • Reason codes for churn, downgrade, pause or reactivation.
  • Whether amount is invoiced, collected or contracted; taxes; the unit and scale of S&M.
  • Customer size, geography or any segmentation beyond industry; history before {{window_start}} and after {{window_end}}.
